38.8% of flight delay is not caused by
the flight that reports it. It's inherited.

Chaining 33.7 million flights into the rotations each aircraft actually flew separates delay that originated with a flight from delay that merely arrived on it — and the distinction changes where it's worth intervening.

Finding 1

Where the delay minutes actually come from

Of 470.7 million delay minutes in five years, more than a third arrived on an aircraft that was already late. Inherited delay has no cause of its own — it was created somewhere else and carried here by an airframe, which means it cannot be fixed at the flight that reports it.

Finding 2

The mechanism is slack — and the model can be checked

Delay passes through a turn only to the extent the ground time can't absorb it: predicted = max(0, inbound_delay − slack). BTS separately requires airlines to report late-aircraft minutes, so the feed already holds the answer, arrived at a different way.

Turns receiving an aircraft 30–60 minutes late

More scheduled slack, less delay handed onward. The 60–89 minute bars break the pattern — see the note below.

The relationship is strong but not monotonic. The 60–89 minute buckets sit above the 45–59 bucket, because turns scheduled 100–120 minutes are disproportionately Southwest (25% of that bucket against 14% of the one below) and disproportionately already mid-cascade (58% against 32%). Restricting to turns not already deep in a cascade narrows the bump without removing it, so it is reported rather than controlled away.

Findings 3 & 4

A morning delay costs 13× more — but buffering the morning doesn't work

These two charts share an x-axis and disagree completely, which is the whole finding. Damage from a root delay peaks in the morning. The value of adding schedule buffer peaks in the evening. They are not the same lever, and the hours where it would help most are the hours where it does least.

How far a single delay travels

Most cascades stop after one leg. The tail is what costs money: a chain that reaches four legs carries roughly four times the downstream minutes of one that stops at the first.

Finding 6

The carrier table, and what sits underneath it

Inherited share explains the ranking better than the ranking describes itself. It is not a quality gap — it's a business model. High aircraft utilisation is how the low-cost fare exists; the cascade is what it costs.

Finding 7

December 2022, with a control group

A winter storm hit on 21–23 December. Every carrier flew into the same weather, which makes what followed a natural experiment — the storm is the treatment and the other carriers are the control.

Where to intervene first

The propagation model ranked 1,241 carrier–station combinations by the delay a targeted buffer would absorb. Read rank 5 against rank 1: Delta at Atlanta runs more than twice Southwest's Denver turn volume and would save a third as many minutes, because it already schedules 40 minutes of median slack against Southwest's 13. Atlanta doesn't have a turn problem. Denver does.
